Stars Have to Star, But That's Not Enough

I've said it before and I'll say it again: the stars have to star. Trevor Lawrence has to be Trevor Lawrence. Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen have to be game wreckers. Travis Hunter has to be Travis Hunter.

But that's only half the equation. Because championship teams also get impact from players who don't always get the credit. Players at positions where the work doesn't show up on the highlight reel. Players whose value you only notice when they're gone.

And that brings us right to the first big story.

Huge News Number One: Jordan Davis and the Invisible Job

Jordan Davis plays a position where you often don't realize how important his impact is while you're watching the game. I used to say the exact same thing about DaVon Hamilton before he got hurt.

Think about it. When a team can't run the football, there's a very good chance the reason has a lot to do with how well the nose tackle is playing. But you're not going to see a bunch of splash plays from that guy. You're not going to see him on SportsCenter every week. He's taking on double teams, holding his ground, clogging lanes, and making it possible for everybody else to look great.

So when a team gives up a first-round pick and a second-round pick for that kind of player, you can understand why there's criticism. People want to see something for that price. They want fireworks.

Against the Giants, Jordan Davis played roughly half the snaps. And here's what a lot of people missed: when he was on the field, the Giants couldn't run the football. Their rushing numbers got padded mostly by their quarterback, an athletic runner who escaped some pressure and found holes in the Jaguars' defense. But the traditional run game? When Davis was in there, it went nowhere.

And earlier this week, we talked about that one play where the Giants doubled Davis and Davon Godchaux, and Ventrell Miller came free and absolutely blew up Cam Skattebo in the backfield. That's the job. That's exactly what Jordan Davis was brought here to do. If you go back and break down the tape, you can't point to a single play where he got wiped out. Not one.

Is the Juice Worth the Squeeze?

Now, the question people are going to keep asking is simple: was the juice worth the squeeze?

And here's where perception gets really interesting. DaVon Hamilton also played about half the snaps. And when he was healthy, we were all bragging about him like he was an All-Pro. Remember? I said he was a top-three nose tackle in the league. People loved him.

So why does it feel different when Jordan Davis plays half the snaps?

Because of the cost of acquisition. DaVon Hamilton was a homegrown player. He was a third-round pick. You didn't have to give up anything to get him. Jordan Davis cost a first and a second. And when you pay a big price, people expect a big show.

But think about this. What if the numbers say Jordan Davis played basically the same way DaVon Hamilton plays? What if there's very little difference at all? Then the player isn't the problem. The expectations are.

And remember the context. Davis just got here. He was banged up when he arrived. He never had a bye week this season. And he's learning a brand-new defense with new terminology, new calls, and new teammates. It's a lot to absorb.

Winning Ends Every Argument

And here's the bottom line, and I want every Jaguars fan to really hear this. The moment you stop arguing about the price is the moment you start winning.

Nobody cares where a player was drafted. Nobody cares what you traded for him. Nobody cares about his stat line. Not if your team wins. Period. Point blank. End of discussion.

But when you lose, even if the player doesn't play poorly, people are going to call it a waste of money. A waste of draft capital. They'll say that if you hadn't made that trade, you could have used those resources to fix the areas where you're truly weak. And honestly, sometimes that argument is weak in and of itself. But that's how the conversation goes when the scoreboard doesn't cooperate.

So yes, I want to see more from Jordan Davis. We all do. And I know "more" might mean a sack. That's not why he was brought here, but it would absolutely help. And sometimes "more" just means the guys around him start playing better too.

Why He Looked Different in Philadelphia

Now, a lot of people are saying, "When I watched him with the Eagles, I noticed him every week. Why don't I notice him the same way here?"

And that's a fair question. But let's think about why he stood out in Philadelphia.

He was playing well, sure. But the guy next to him was great. And the guy next to that guy was great. The players behind them were great. Most of the secondary was great. The defensive coordinator was great. And on the other side of the ball, the offensive line was dominant. That meant Philadelphia was usually playing with a lead. And when you're playing from in front, the other offense becomes predictable. They have to throw. And when the opponent is predictable, the defense gets a chance to look even better.

That formula is exactly what the Jaguars want to build. Score early, play from in front, and let this front seven pin its ears back.

Now, the one big difference is the offensive line. This Jaguars line isn't going to dominate the way those Eagles lines did, at least not right now, not this season. But the Jaguars have something those Eagles didn't. They're a more explosive offense. Because in my opinion, Trevor Lawrence is a better quarterback than Jalen Hurts. It's not exactly apples to apples, but the comparison is always going to be there. And if this offense starts fast, Jordan Davis is going to be noticed a lot more.

You can look at the analytics. You can look at PFF. You can look at all the charting data in the world. But the bottom line is simple: they've got to do better. And I believe they will.

The Guys Around Him Have to Step Up

And that brings me to something bigger. It's not just about Jordan Davis. It's about everybody around him.

Travon Walker said in an interview this week that he's not playing his best, but he's not playing terrible either. And he mentioned he's a little banged up. That sent a lot of people running to the injury report. But you probably won't find it there.

Here's the reality. Every player in the NFL is hurt at this point in the season. Every single one. Bumps, bruises, sprains, things that never show up on any report. And honestly, a lot of us would call out of work with half of what these guys play through. I believe Travon when he says something is holding him back a little. And Josh Hines-Allen looks like he might be dealing with something too.

But when the guys around Jordan Davis play better, Davis is going to look better. And when Davis plays better, they're going to look better. That's how a defensive line works. It's all connected.

Does BJ Green Need to Come Back Now?

And that leads to a question a lot of fans are asking: do the Jaguars need BJ Green back right now?

Do they need him to bring the pop everybody thought this pass rush would have? Because there have been a few guys who, while they haven't played poorly, haven't popped the way we expected. Dennis Gardeck hasn't popped like we hoped. Danny Striggow hasn't popped like we hoped.

These are guys who have built their careers as classic overachievers. And at some point, they need to start overachieving again, because it lifts everybody around them. Wesley Williams got in on some action against the Giants, and that's great. We want to see more of that.

BJ Green's return window is open. He's not the long, twitchy kind of edge rusher, but he knows how to find his way to the quarterback, especially on third down. And right now, this defense could really use that.

From Floor to Ceiling

And here's a point I really want to drive home. A lot of these players have a high floor. They're reliable. They're dependable. They rarely embarrass you. But at some point, that floor needs to start rising toward a ceiling.

Because you're going to need more than potential. You're going to need more than "he's better than people thought."

Is he better than people thought? Okay, great. But is he good enough to help this team win a Super Bowl? Because at the end of the day, it doesn't matter if a player is better than expected if that version of him isn't good enough to elevate this team and win more football games.

That's the standard now. This isn't a rebuilding team hoping its young players develop. This is a team that traded its 2027 draft to chase a championship. "Better than expected" doesn't cut it anymore.

Huge News Number Two: Jessie Bates Needs His Moment

Now let's talk about Jessie Bates. And I want to be upfront: I've been a big fan of Jessie Bates for a long, long time. I still am. Nothing has changed for me.

But against the Giants, you didn't see the impact you were hoping for. Either the Giants were deliberately avoiding him, or the plays just didn't come his way. And at times, he looked like a guy working really hard to get acclimated to a new system.

And that makes total sense. Remember what Jessie Bates has been through. He landed from Spain, didn't sleep at all, and has been sleeping about five hours a night since the trade. He's stayed at the building late into the night trying to learn this defense. He went straight from Atlanta to Jacksonville without a bye week. And Liam Coen said there were a lot of moving parts in the game against the Giants. I have to wonder if some of what we saw from Bates is part of what Coen was talking about.

But here's why I'm not worried. Jessie Bates has 28 career interceptions and 14 forced fumbles. Since he entered the league in 2018, he's tied for the most interceptions of anyone in the NFL. That doesn't disappear because of one quiet game. That's who he is.

And I expect a good chunk of plays from him this weekend. Plays that remind everybody exactly why the Jaguars went out and got him.

The Perfect Opponent for a Breakout

This weekend, the Jaguars face the Tennessee Titans again. And there's something to remember about that matchup. When the Jaguars played the Titans earlier this month, Jessie Bates and Jordan Davis didn't even suit up. Liam Coen held them out to rest them and give them time to learn the defense.

So this is their first crack at Tennessee in teal and black. And the Titans are a banged-up, struggling team. They've traded away some of their best defenders. Their quarterback, Cam Ward, is going to be under pressure. And when a young quarterback is under pressure, the ball comes out early and off target.

That's exactly where Jessie Bates makes his living. That's exactly where Travis Hunter makes his living. And it's exactly what Travis Hunter was talking about a couple of weeks ago: push the pocket, force bad throws, and let the secondary take the ball away.

Jessie Bates is one of the best free safeties in the National Football League. This weekend, let's see him prove it for the Jacksonville Jaguars.

Huge News Number Three: We're Still Waiting on Brian Thomas Jr.

And now, the third big story. The one that might matter most of all. Brian Thomas Jr.

We're still waiting. Every Jaguars fan knows it. And I think Brian Thomas Jr. knows it too.

Let's go back to the trade deadline. There was a lot of speculation that he could be moved. I even said I thought there was a real chance. Against Baltimore, he barely factored into the game. He only played around 18 snaps. And that was right after a week where he'd scored a touchdown against the Colts and was giving great interviews.

But the Jaguars kept him. And Liam Coen and the coaching staff came out and said, very clearly, that he's going to be an important part of the second half of the season. It would have taken a huge offer to pry him loose, and nobody made it.

So now the question is simple. If he's going to be a big part of the second half, when does that start?

Because think about what this offense looks like when Brian Thomas Jr. is rolling. Parker Washington leads the team in receiving and has been playing like a top-tier receiver. Jakobi Meyers is a reliable veteran. Travis Hunter is a weapon. The tight end room is deep. Bhayshul Tuten is a threat out of the backfield. And Trevor Lawrence has been playing at an MVP level, leading the league in yards per attempt.

Now add a fully engaged, explosive Brian Thomas Jr. stretching the field on the outside. Defenses can't double Parker. They can't sit on the short stuff. They have to respect the deep ball on every single snap. That's when this offense goes from very good to unstoppable.

Why BTJ Is the Key to Playing From in Front

And here's how all three of these stories connect. Remember what I said about the Eagles formula? Play from in front. Make the other offense predictable. Let the defense attack.

Brian Thomas Jr. is one of the best tools this team has to play from in front. One deep shot early in the game, one big play on the first drive, and suddenly the Jaguars are up seven or fourteen points. And now the Titans have to throw. And now Jordan Davis and the Duval Wall can pin their ears back. And now Cam Ward is throwing into a secondary with Jessie Bates and Travis Hunter waiting.

Do you see it? It's all connected. BTJ creates the lead. The lead creates predictability. Predictability unleashes the defense. And the defense makes Jordan Davis and Jessie Bates look exactly like the players the Jaguars traded for.

Liam Coen fixed the slow-start problem this season. Last year, the Jaguars scored on their opening drive just three times in 18 games. This year, they did it three times in their first eight. The coaches give the players a short list of calls to hunt for on that first drive. I would love to see a deep shot to Brian Thomas Jr. on that list this weekend.

And honestly, this is the perfect week for it. There are only 17 regular-season games. Every one of them matters, especially now, with the Jaguars coming off a loss and fighting for the top seed in the AFC. A big game against a division rival could be the spark that gets Brian Thomas Jr. on a real run heading into December.

Why the Giants Loss Doesn't Change the Big Picture

Now, let's take a step back for a second. Yes, the Jaguars lost to the Giants. Yes, it stings. And yes, there was that Parker Washington play that a lot of people believe should have been a touchdown.

But one loss doesn't erase what this team is. The Jaguars still have the best point differential in the league. They still lead the AFC South. They still lead the NFL in turnover margin. Trevor Lawrence is still playing at an MVP level. And the defensive line, as we talked about this week, was the best unit on the field against the Giants. Travon Walker had seven pressures. Josh Hines-Allen had a sack, a forced fumble, and a pass breakup.

What the loss does is raise the urgency. The Jaguars are chasing home-field advantage throughout the playoffs for the first time since 1999. Every game matters now. And you can't afford to drop games against teams you're supposed to beat.

That's why this weekend is so important. Not just to win, but to play to the standard. To get the new pieces working. To get BTJ involved. And to remind the whole league that the loss to the Giants was a bump in the road, not a sign of things to come.

Never Forget 1999

And of course, it's the Titans. You know how I feel about this. In 1999, the Jaguars went 15 and 3, and all three of those losses came against Tennessee. Every Jaguars fan who lived through that season remembers it.

So whenever you have a chance to step on the Titans, you do it. Especially after a loss. Especially when you need to get back on track. Especially when you've got new stars ready to make their first impression against this rival.

Final Thoughts

So let's wrap up this triple dose of huge news.

Number one: Jordan Davis. He's doing his job, even if it doesn't always show up on the highlight reel. When he was on the field against the Giants, they couldn't run the ball. But the Jaguars need more from him, and from the guys around him, to justify the price and win games.

Number two: Jessie Bates. A quiet first outing, but one of the best ball hawks of the last decade. This weekend, against a quarterback who's going to be under pressure, I expect him to remind everyone why Jacksonville went and got him.

And number three: Brian Thomas Jr. The coaching staff says he's a big part of the second half. Now it's time to show it. And if he gets going, everything else about this team gets easier.

The stars have to star. But this weekend, the Jaguars need impact from everybody. And I believe they're going to get it.
