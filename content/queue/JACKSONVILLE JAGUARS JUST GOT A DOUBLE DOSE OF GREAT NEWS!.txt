Great News Number One: Patrick Mekari's Window Is Open

Let's start with the offensive line, because this is the news that could matter most down the stretch.

Back in mid-August, it was reported that Patrick Mekari had undergone a procedure on his back. The Jaguars placed him on injured reserve with a designation to return. And now, the team has officially opened his practice window. That means Jacksonville has 21 days to add him back to the 53-man roster.

And with Mekari out, the line has had to shuffle. Ezra Cleveland moved over to right guard, and rookie Emmanuel Pregnon has been starting at left guard. So it's going to be really interesting to see where Mekari fits once he's back. Does he slide in at guard? Does he give them flexibility at tackle? Does he become the swing piece that keeps this line from falling apart every time somebody gets hurt?

Whatever role he ends up in, I believe he's going to give this line a real boost. And this line needs it.

The Offensive Line Is Priority Number One

I'm going to be honest with you. Right now, the offensive line is the most underperforming unit on this football team. Not the worst in talent, but the furthest from what it could be. I truly believe this group can be better than it currently is.

Will it get there? Will it reach its potential? I don't know for sure. But with this coaching staff, I believe that whatever is in there, they can pull it out.

And here's why it matters so much. If you have championship aspirations, if a Super Bowl is the goal, this offensive line absolutely has to be better. Because we've all seen what happens when Trevor Lawrence gets time. He's leading the league in yards per attempt. He's playing at an MVP level. When he's clean, he picks defenses apart.

So in my opinion, the offensive line is the single most important thing on this team right now. More important than the pass rush. More important than the run game. More important than anything else going on in that building.

And especially against elite defensive linemen. This group has struggled with speed off the edge. Fast, bendy pass rushers have given them problems all year. And in January, you're going to see those guys every single week. Buffalo, Kansas City, Pittsburgh, Denver. You have to be able to hold up.

And think about what this line has been through. They've been missing their center. They were missing their left tackle. Then the backup left tackle went down. Protect Bhayshul Tuten From Himself

Here's my message to the Jaguars this week: you have to protect Bhayshul Tuten from himself.

Because he's not going to stop. Everybody's said it. He's determined to play. He's determined to be on that field every single Sunday, and he's going to do whatever it takes to get out there. And I love that about him. That's the mentality you want. If I can walk, I can play. That's a football player.

But the only way he's truly going to get healthy is if he's not getting hammered every week. And at this point, it's on the coaches to make that decision for him.

Now, I'm not saying you deactivate him. I've seen some people suggest the Jaguars should sit him down completely. I don't go that far. If he's ready on Sunday, if he's got full range of motion, if he can be effective, then of course you want him out there. But you manage him. You limit him. You protect him.

And let's remember what happened in the first Titans game. Tuten got 26 carries after not practicing for most of the week. Twenty-six. I didn't like that. I don't like it at all. Especially when you've got a capable back sitting right there.

And it's clearly affected him. He got stepped on twice, and yes, they're saying he feels a little better. But this is the kind of injury that lingers. Chris Rodriguez Jr.'s hand is going to linger a bit too, but it isn't affecting how he runs. With Tuten, it obviously is.

And watch what happened against the Giants. When Tuten got hurt, the Jaguars went away from the run. You could see it. And I know Grant Udinski saw it. I know Liam Coen saw it. And one thing this staff is really good at is adjusting. Adding things to a player's plate when he can handle more, and taking things off when he can't. How This Fits the Bigger Picture

Now, let's connect the dots, because all of this ties together.

The run game has been a big part of this team's identity. Coming into the last few weeks, the Jaguars were averaging 117.1 rushing yards per game, in the top half of the league, and both Tuten and Rodriguez were averaging more than four yards a carry. That balance is what makes this offense so hard to defend. You can't sit back against Trevor Lawrence, because they'll pound you on the ground. And you can't stack the box, because Trevor will carve you up through the air.

But lately, that balance has slipped. Part of it is the offensive line, which has been dealing with injuries and inconsistency in both pass protection and run blocking. Part of it is Tuten not being himself. And when the run game slips, the whole offense becomes more predictable.

That's why both pieces of great news matter so much. Patrick Mekari coming back helps the line. A healthy rotation with Rodriguez taking the lead helps the run game. And when the run game works, the play-action works. And when play-action works, Trevor Lawrence gets those big throws downfield to Parker Washington, Brian Thomas Jr., Jakobi Meyers, and Travis Hunter.

And on the other side, BJ Green coming back helps the pass rush. With Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen already winning their matchups and the Duval Wall demanding double teams inside, Green could be the final piece that turns pressure into sacks.

This is how you rebound from a loss. You get healthier. You get smarter. And you get back to the formula that got you here.

Growing Pains Are Part of the Process

But here's the thing. The loss to the Giants showed us something too. It showed the difference between a coach with nearly two decades of experience and a Super Bowl ring, John Harbaugh, and a coach in only his second year as a head coach.

And that's not a knock on Liam Coen. Not at all. As brilliant as he is, experience has to be earned. You can't skip it. He has to go through things, just like Trevor Lawrence had to go through things.

And I think people forget that part about Trevor. Everybody wants to give Liam all the credit. "Oh, without Liam, Trevor would be this or that." But Trevor doesn't get to this level without everything he went through before Liam got here. The tough years. The coaching changes. The criticism. All of that shaped him into the quarterback he is today.

And now Liam has to go through some of that himself. He's a first-time head coach. He's never done this before. He has to learn. He has to develop a deeper feel for the game, for in-game management, for those critical moments when everything is on the line. And as time goes on, he's going to have more and more of that feel.

Watching John Harbaugh operate on Sunday, it was like clockwork. Everything was routine. Military style. Five a.m., we're doing this. Six a.m., we're doing that. Every detail planned. Every situation accounted for. That's what twenty years of head coaching gives you.

Liam Coen is going to get there. And honestly, the scary part for the rest of the league is that he's already this good while still learning. Imagine what he looks like in year three, year four, year five, when he's got that same routine, that same feel, that same instinct for the big moments.

This team isn't peaking. It's growing. And the man leading it is growing right along with it.
