The Duval Wall: Size, Power, and a Plan

Let’s start with the numbers, because the numbers tell a big part of this story. And I mean big.

DaVon Hamilton is a huge human being. Six-foot-four, 335 pounds. Before he got hurt, I would have told you he was a top-three nose tackle in the entire National Football League. One of the best run stoppers in the game. And I still feel that way.

Now you bring in Jordan Davis from Philadelphia. And Jordan Davis is right in that same territory in terms of where he ranks among interior defenders. But he’s even bigger. Jordan Davis is six-foot-six and around 350 pounds. That is an enormous individual.

But here’s what I really want you to understand. Jordan Davis isn’t just big for the sake of being big. He’s not some guy who just takes up space and eats blocks. He has juice. He moves in a way that a man that size really shouldn’t be able to move. And I genuinely believe this coaching staff has a specific plan for him. I think they’re going to use him a little differently than he was used in Philly, and a little differently than you’ve seen this team use a nose tackle before.

The wall is built. And it almost feels like something out of Game of Thrones. That mindset of building a wall and saying, “Nothing gets through here.” That’s who this team wants to be. They don’t want to give up anything up the middle. Not one yard.

And speaking of not giving up a yard, I love what Liam Coen shared in his press conference. Coen said the overall feeling in the building is simple: let’s go. That’s it. And then he told a story about his time at Kentucky. He remembered watching Jordan Davis walk the field before a game and thinking, basically, how are we going to get even one yard against this guy? That’s the kind of presence Jordan Davis has. Opposing coaches see him before kickoff and start rethinking their entire run game.

Some of you remember when Jacksonville had “Sacksonville” back in 2017. Well, this is the Duval Wall. That’s what I’m calling it.

And look at the rest of the group around him. Arik Armstead at six-foot-seven, somewhere around 290 to 295 pounds. Travon Walker at six-foot-five, 275. Josh Hines-Allen at six-foot-four, 265. And there’s young talent coming in behind them too. This is a big, physical football team. A big, physical football team.

The Linebackers Have to Be Celebrating

Now, if there’s anybody who didn’t sleep the night these moves came through, outside of defensive coordinator Anthony Campanile, it had to be the off-ball linebackers. I’m telling you, those guys had to feel like it was Christmas morning.

Because think about what this does for them. When you’ve got Jordan Davis and Arik Armstead eating up blocks in the middle, the offensive linemen can’t get to the second level. That keeps everything clean for the linebackers in the run game. And I mean clean. It’s not going to make sense how free they’re going to be.

You’re going to see Ventrell Miller and Foye Oluokun just firing downhill, meeting running backs in the hole, flying sideline to sideline without a guard climbing up to bury them. And it’s going to give Anthony Campanile a whole bunch of new ways to call this defense. More blitz options. More disguises. More freedom to be creative, because the foundation up front is so solid.

This is huge. No pun intended. It is huge for the Jacksonville Jaguars as they move forward through the rest of this season.

Why These Moves Are Sustainable for Years

There’s been so much talk about contracts and draft capital this week. And honestly, it drives me a little crazy when people don’t take a deeper look at it. But that’s what we’re here for on Jaguars Floor. So let’s take that deep dive together.

On the surface, a lot of people look at these trades and think the Jaguars overpaid with those picks. So let’s break that down.

First of all, a first-round pick from a playoff team is not the same thing as a first-round pick from a terrible team. A bad team is picking in the top ten, maybe even the top seven. A playoff team is picking late. Those picks are not the same.

It’s like someone saying they make six figures. Okay, but do you make 102,000 dollars, or do you make 940,000 dollars? Technically both are six figures, but there’s a massive difference. That’s the same thing with first-round picks. A late first is not a top-ten pick, and people need to stop treating them like they’re equal.

Second, the Jaguars are getting quality players for these picks, and they don’t have to wait through the development process. When you draft a player, even a good one, you often have to wait a year, two years, sometimes more before he becomes the player you hoped for. With Jordan Davis, there’s no waiting. He’s under contract for the next three and a half years. You know exactly who he is, what he is, and what he’s going to give you. He’s young. And somebody else already paid him a whole bunch of bonus money.

A New Way to Build: Trades Over Free Agency

Think back to this past offseason. A lot of people were frustrated with how quiet the Jaguars were in free agency. Part of that was because of dead money on the books from decisions made before the current regime took over. That’s just the reality of cleaning up somebody else’s situation.

But the rest of it? Maybe this front office just views things differently. Instead of overpaying players on the open market in free agency, where you’re competing against 31 other teams and driving up the price, they acquire players at the trade deadline, where they never have to hand out a big pile of cash up front, unless they decide to re-sign them.

Look at what they did with Jakobi Meyers. They didn’t get him in the offseason. They traded for him during the season, and then they worked out a contract extension before he ever hit free agency. They never had to compete against a bunch of other teams bidding on him. That’s smart. That’s a new strategy. And it takes some of us a little time to look past what we’re used to.

Because we’re used to the idea that you covet your draft picks. You hoard them. You collect as many as you can so you can have all that excitement during those three days in April. And I get it. I understand the appeal of homegrown talent.

But remember what this regime did the very first time they had a chance? They traded two picks to move up and get Travis Hunter. They went and got a player they never would have landed if they hadn’t made that move.

And what they’re doing now is a different version of the same idea. They’re trading picks for proven players who are already under contract.

And not only is money coming off the books, the salary cap is going up. The cap goes up every single year. So the Jaguars are going to be in really, really good financial shape moving forward.

Gone are the days of this franchise chasing new stars every offseason. They know who their people are. And their people are young. Travis Hunter. Trevor Lawrence. Travon Walker. Anton Harrison. They still have plans for Brian Thomas Jr. All of these players are in their mid-twenties. Outside of maybe Trevor, I don’t think anybody I just named is even 26 years old.

This is sustainable. This is not a team throwing picks around and reaching for anything that moves. The Jaguars have a window, and that window may be much bigger than three years. With this core, that window could be five, six, maybe even seven years.

And I have full confidence that they’re going to keep building and adding, because of what they’ve shown they can do on day two and day three of the draft. They identify talent really well.

The Surprise Move: BJ Green’s Window Is Open

And now let’s get to the news that adds even more fuel to this fire. The Jaguars are about to get some help on the pass rush.

At the deadline, the Jaguars didn’t directly address the edge rusher position with a trade. From what I’ve heard, they did put in some inquiries with the New York Jets about Will McDonald, but the Jets weren’t going to move him. And that’s fine. Because Jacksonville has help coming from inside the building.

Liam Coen confirmed that BJ Green’s return window is open. That means he can start working his way back toward the field. I don’t know if he’ll be ready this week, but it’s going to be soon. And once he gets himself back into football shape, he’s going to be a real asset for this team during the stretch run.

And the Jaguars are going to need all hands on deck. Let me be clear about this. Wherever you think this team is going, however high you think their ceiling is, it’s not going to be easy. Nobody is going to roll over for the Jaguars just because they made some big trades. Every team on the schedule is going to give them their best shot, especially now that they’ve got a target on their back.

That’s why getting BJ Green back is so important. And BJ Green is a different kind of pass rusher. He’s not built like your traditional long, rangy, twitchy edge rusher. But what he is, and who he is, is a guy who knows how to find a way to the quarterback. Especially on third down. He’s crafty. He’s relentless. He figures it out.

Picture this. You’ve got the Duval Wall in the middle with Jordan Davis and Arik Armstead collapsing the pocket. You’ve got Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen coming off the edges. And on third down, you add BJ Green into the rotation as another player who just knows how to get home. Where is the quarterback supposed to go? There’s nowhere to step up. There’s nowhere to escape. That’s what this defense could look like by December.
