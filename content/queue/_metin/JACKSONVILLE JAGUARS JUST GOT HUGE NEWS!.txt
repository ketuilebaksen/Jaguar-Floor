The Duval Wall Did Its Job

And now let's talk about the big men in the middle. The Duval Wall. Jordan Davis and Davon Godchaux.

Remember why the Jaguars made those trades in the first place. DaVon Hamilton went down for the season, and the run defense dropped from first in the league to fifth almost overnight. The front office went out and spent a first-round pick, two second-rounders, and more to rebuild this defense in one afternoon. Godchaux came from New Orleans for a sixth-round pick. Davis came from Philadelphia for a first and a second.

And on Sunday, they did exactly what they were brought here to do. They ate up blockers. They took on double teams. They occupied space so that everybody behind them could play fast and free.

And there was one play that showed it perfectly. The Giants' Cam Skattebo took a handoff, and the offensive line committed double teams to Jordan Davis and Davon Godchaux. That left Ventrell Miller completely free, with nobody climbing up to block him. And Miller shot straight through the hole and absolutely splattered Skattebo in the backfield. Bam.

That's the Duval Wall in action. That's the whole plan. When you have two massive human beings in the middle who demand double teams, somebody else comes free. And when that somebody is Ventrell Miller, a linebacker people are now comparing to Fred Warner, the ball carrier is in serious trouble.

And that's exactly what we're going to keep seeing. Because when offensive lines have to double team Davis and Godchaux, they can't double team Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen. That means one-on-ones on the edges. And Walker and Hines-Allen are going to keep winning those one-on-ones. Sunday was proof of that.
