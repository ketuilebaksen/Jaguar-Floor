Superman Got Banged Up

Let's be clear about something first. Outside of Trevor Lawrence, Parker Washington has been the best player on the Jacksonville Jaguars this season. Period.

He's picked up right where he left off last year. He leads the team in receiving. He's found the end zone multiple times. He makes contested catches look easy. His body control is ridiculous. And earlier this week, I told you I believe he's a top-five wide receiver in the National Football League, a player who deserves 30-plus million dollars a year and should be paid right now.

He's been Superman for this offense.

Well, Superman got banged up.

And it happened at a strange time. During the season, practice rules are completely different from training camp. Most of the time there isn't heavy contact. So when a player gets hurt in practice late in the week, in November, people start asking questions. How did it happen? Was it a walkthrough? Was it a freak accident?

And those are fair questions. But let's be real about one thing: nobody in that building is trying to get anybody hurt. Sometimes things just happen in football. It's a violent, unpredictable game, even on a Thursday afternoon.

What to Listen for From Liam Coen

Now, here's my warning to every Jaguars fan before Liam Coen steps to that podium. Don't overreact to the wording. Either way.

The Jaguars are very, very careful about how they release injury information. Very precautionary. And sometimes that means an injury sounds minor when it isn't.

Remember Travis Hunter last year. When he got hurt, it sounded just like this. Nothing alarming. Nothing dramatic. And then it turned out he had to miss the rest of the season.

But it can go the other way too. Sometimes something sounds scary and ends up being nothing. A guy is limited for a day, gets treatment, and he's out there on Sunday catching touchdowns like nothing happened.

So when Coen speaks, listen closely. Does he give a timeline? Does he say "day-to-day"? Does he mention more tests? Does he dodge the question entirely? Those little details will tell you a lot more than one word on the injury report.

And look, if it's minor, great. We keep pushing. Players play through injuries every single week in the NFL. But if it's serious, that changes the conversation for the rest of the season.

Next Man Up Only Works for So Long

And here's what makes this so concerning. It's not just one injury. It's the pile.

"Next man up" is a great phrase. Every team says it. But next man up, then next man up, then next man up after that? At some point you run out of next men. Or at least you run out of next men who are good enough to make the same impact as the people they're replacing.

Look at what this team has already been through. They lost their starting left tackle. Then his backup got banged up. They lost their center. They lost their nose tackle, DaVon Hamilton, for the season, and had to make moves at the trade deadline to rebuild a position they thought was already a strength. They lost Eric Murray for the year too.

Last year it was Travis Hunter during a 13-win season. Now it could be Parker Washington.

And injuries are the one thing that has been rearing its ugly head for this team as the season wears on. Back when people were talking about a 15 and 2 season, I said the only thing that could really slow this team down was injuries. That's exactly what we're seeing now.

Can the Jaguars Beat the Titans Without Parker?

So let's talk short term. If Parker Washington can't go on Sunday, can the Jaguars still beat the Tennessee Titans?

Should they? Absolutely. The Titans are one and nine. The Jaguars should be able to beat them without Parker Washington. Honestly, they should be able to beat them without a whole bunch of people.

But saying it and proving it are two very different things.

Remember just two weeks ago. When the Jaguars played this same Tennessee team, it was a one-score game with eight minutes left. One score! Against a one-win team. That should tell you everything you need to know about how divisional games work.

And this is a rivalry game. You know how I feel about the Titans. We never forget 1999, when the Jaguars went 15 and 3 and every single one of those losses came against Tennessee. Bad records don't matter in these games. The Titans would love nothing more than to ruin Jacksonville's season.

The good news? The Jaguars are coming home for the first time in five or six weeks. A home crowd, a familiar locker room, their own sideline. That matters. But it's still not the situation you want to be in, thinking about your best receiver's shoulder heading into a must-win game.

Who Steps Up at Receiver?

So let's say Parker doesn't play Sunday. Without knowing if it's a short-term or long-term injury, do the Jaguars have enough?

Yes. But a lot of people are going to need to step up.

Start with Brian Thomas Jr. This is the moment. We've been saying it for weeks: we're still waiting on Brian Thomas Jr. The coaching staff said he'd be a big part of the second half of the season. They kept him at the trade deadline when plenty of people thought he'd be gone. Well, if Parker is out, there's no more waiting. This is his opportunity to become the focal point of the passing game. And honestly, maybe this is exactly what he needs to get rolling.

Then there's Jakobi Meyers. He's a reliable veteran, a guy Trevor trusts. But he's also on the injury report, limited with an ankle. So the Jaguars can't just assume he's going to carry the load either.

Maybe CJ Williams finally gets activated. Maybe they lean more on Josh Cameron. Those are guys who've been waiting for a chance, and opportunity is knocking.

And then there's the most exciting option of all: a heavier dose of Travis Hunter on offense. We know what he can do. He's already had a game this season where he picked off a pass and scored a touchdown in the same afternoon. If Parker is out, Liam Coen and Grant Udinski could expand Hunter's offensive role, move him around, scheme him open, and let him be the two-way star the Jaguars traded up to get.

And let's not forget the tight ends. Remember all that criticism about how many tight ends this team drafted? A third of Jacksonville's snaps include two or three tight ends, and every one of them is a real threat. We saw it against Baltimore when they opened the game hitting Tanner Koziol, then Nate Boerkircher dragging defenders on the very next play. If Parker can't go, this could be the week that tight end room really earns its keep.

The Trevor Lawrence Problem Nobody Talks About

Now here's where it gets tricky. Losing Parker Washington puts more of the load back on Trevor Lawrence. And there's a stat that worries me.

Over the last couple of years, when Trevor Lawrence throws the ball more than 40 times in a game, the Jaguars are one and five.

Now, let me be very clear about what that means and what it doesn't mean. It doesn't mean you can't depend on your quarterback. It doesn't mean you take the ball out of Trevor's hands. Trevor is playing at an MVP level. He's got 18 touchdowns, a 111.7 passer rating, and he leads the league in yards per attempt.

What it means is this: you cannot become one-dimensional and predictable when your offensive line is in shambles.

That's the real issue. It's not that throwing 40 times causes losses. It's that Trevor usually ends up throwing 40-plus times because the Jaguars are behind. Something got away from them in the game plan, and now they have to speed up, chase points, and abandon the run. And when that happens, opposing pass rushers pin their ears back against an offensive line that's already been struggling, especially against speed off the edge.

Could the Jaguars come out with a plan for Trevor to throw it 40 times and win? Sure. But it's not the best path to victory for this team. This team wins with complementary football. A balanced offense, a defense that leads the league in turnover margin, and a run game that keeps Trevor in manageable situations.

The Run Game Has to Show Up

And that's the problem. The Jaguars have struggled to run the football over the last two weeks. And losing Parker makes it even more important that they figure it out.

Which brings us to Bhayshul Tuten.

Why Bhayshul Tuten Should Get Sunday Off

Bhayshul Tuten needs rest. Real rest.

Some people thought he should have sat out the first Titans game two weeks ago. Instead, he got 26 carries after missing most of the week of practice. Then came the Giants game, sandwiched right between the two Titans matchups. It was a big game, and they ran him and ran him and ran him. And it seemed like every few plays he was getting stepped on, banged up, and hurt all over again.

He's dealing with an ankle and foot issue. He didn't practice. And as much as he wants to play, and he absolutely does because that's the kind of competitor he is, the only way he's truly going to get healthy is to get off the field.

And honestly, the case for resting him is even stronger now. Think about it. The Jaguars are facing a one and nine team at home. If there's ever a week to give Tuten a break, this is it. You want him healthy for December and January, not limping into the playoffs because you squeezed every carry out of him in November.

Hand the job to Chris Rodriguez Jr. He's healthy. He was a full participant despite his hand injury, and that hand isn't affecting how he runs. He's a rhythm runner who gets stronger as the game goes on. Give him 20-plus carries, let him wear down the Titans' defense, and let him close it out in the fourth quarter.

Whether Tuten is fully inactive or just heavily limited, the message is the same. Protect him now so you have him later.

Finally, Some Good Injury News: Patrick Mekari

And now, for once, some good injury news. Patrick Mekari's practice window is officially open.

Back in mid-August, it was reported that Mekari had undergone a back procedure. He's been on injured reserve with a designation to return. Now the Jaguars have 21 days to add him back to the 53-man roster.

And this could be huge for an offensive line that badly needs help. With Mekari out, Ezra Cleveland moved to right guard and rookie Emmanuel Pregnon has been starting at left guard, and Pregnon has given up some pressure. Getting a steady veteran back into that mix could stabilize the whole group.

And it ties directly into everything we just talked about. If Parker is out, the Jaguars need balance. If they need balance, they need to run the ball. And if they need to run the ball and protect Trevor, they need a better offensive line. Mekari can help with all of it.

And don't forget BJ Green, who was a full participant in practice. Help is coming on both sides of the ball. The cavalry isn't fully here yet, but it's on the way.

The AFC Playoff Picture

Now let's zoom out and look at the big picture, because the AFC race is getting really interesting.

The Jaguars are seven and three. They'd love to get to eight and three on Sunday. And the AFC South? That's not really an issue. Jacksonville holds a three-game lead over the Houston Texans, who are four and six. The division title is well within reach.

But the top seed is a different story. The Buffalo Bills are off and running. They're 10 and 1. They won again this week, and they keep finding ways to win games late and ugly. They just keep figuring it out. And they don't play in a very tough division.

So even if the Jaguars take care of business Sunday, they're still two full games behind Buffalo. And looking at the Bills' schedule, I'm not sure there are two games left that they can't win. Realistically, the road to the Super Bowl might have to run through cold, nasty Buffalo.

The Bengals are sitting at seven and three. Denver is eight and two and plays Pittsburgh. The Chiefs are eight and three. So for now, this is mostly about seeding. The number two seed is wide open.

And that's the goal now. Separate from the pack. Get ahead of Kansas City. Get ahead of Denver. Get ahead of Cincinnati. Lock up that number two seed and the home playoff games that come with it.

Every team is looking for the easiest road through the playoffs. But it's going to be difficult. And it's going to be a lot more difficult if the Jaguars have to do it for an extended stretch without Parker Washington.

This Is Why You Pay Players

Now, there's one more angle here I want to touch on. If Parker's injury turns out to be serious, this is exactly why you want players to get paid.

Earlier this week, I made the case for the Jaguars to extend Parker Washington right now, during the season. Prep. Prepare. Perform. Produce. Pay Parker.

And honestly, this shouldn't change anything. Whenever the Jaguars decide to sign him, the money shouldn't go up or down because of this. If it's a shoulder issue, it's probably something that heals up well over time. It shouldn't affect his long-term value at all.

But situations like this are a reminder of why players want security. One bad step, one awkward landing, one practice rep, and everything can change. Parker has done everything right. He's earned that contract. And whatever happens with this shoulder, I still believe the Jaguars should lock him up for the long haul.

Never Count This Team Out

And let's remember something important. Last year, the Jaguars lost Travis Hunter for the season. And what did they do? They went on a run and finished 13 and 4 with a division title.

So I'll never count this team out. Not with Trevor Lawrence playing at an MVP level. Not with a defense that leads the league in turnover margin. Not with the Duval Wall in the middle and Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen getting after quarterbacks. And not with Liam Coen, who's 20 and 7 in his first 27 games as head coach.

This team has dealt with adversity before. It has found answers before. And if Parker has to miss time, it will find answers again.
