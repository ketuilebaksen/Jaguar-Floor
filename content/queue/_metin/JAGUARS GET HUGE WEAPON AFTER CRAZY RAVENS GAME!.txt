The Ravens Game: A Sledgehammer Performance

Man, I was up late Thursday night. Real late. And I know a lot of you were right there with me, watching the Jacksonville Jaguars systematically, professionally, methodically take apart the Baltimore Ravens on national television. Final score: 33 to 10. On the road. On a short week. In prime time.

Now, let’s address the obvious right away, because I know the comment section is already loading up. Yes, Lamar Jackson didn’t play. And I’ll be honest with you, if Lamar Jackson is out there, that game probably looks different. It’s probably closer. But you know what? I truly believe the Jaguars still win that football game. And here’s why. This Jaguars team is not playing to the level of its opponent. This Jaguars team is playing to its own standard.

Think about it like this. A sledgehammer is going to do what a sledgehammer does. It doesn’t matter if it’s coming down on a mosquito or a coyote. If something comes at you, you swing with everything you’ve got. That’s what championship-caliber teams do. They don’t look at the other sideline and decide how hard they need to play based on who’s lined up across from them. They play their game, they execute their plan, and they let the scoreboard sort itself out. And that’s exactly what the Jaguars did on Thursday night.

And honestly, the stat sheet doesn’t even do justice to how dominant this performance was. The Ravens got a little bit of something late when Justice Hill broke off a long run, and I know some folks were heated about that. But let’s put that in context. That came late, with a lot of backups on the field. When the starters were out there and the game was actually being decided, this was a one-sided affair from start to finish.

And let’s not skip past how difficult this kind of win really is. This is the NFL. Road wins are hard to come by. Road wins on a short week are even harder. You’ve got less time to prepare, less time to recover, less time to install a game plan and get everybody on the same page. And the Jaguars went into Baltimore and executed basically flawlessly. That is not luck. That is preparation. That is coaching. That is a team that knows exactly who it is.

The Double Standard the Jaguars Always Face

Here’s something that’s always bothered me about the way national media covers certain teams, and I want to get this off my chest because it matters.

When people already believe a team is great, and that team loses a game, they find an excuse. They blame the officials, they blame an injury, they say it was a fluke, they say the team will figure it out. The benefit of the doubt is always there.

But when people have already decided a team isn’t good, every single loss becomes evidence. The Jaguars lost that game in Denver? “See, we told you, they’re not for real.” The Jaguars lost to Houston? “See, there it is, same old Jaguars.”

Come on now. Everybody blows games in Denver. That’s one of the hardest places to play in the entire league. And if you were actually being objective, you could watch those losses and say, “You know what, they came up short, but I see it. I see what this team is building.” Because they were competitive. They were in those games. They lost late, on the road, against good football teams that a lot of people expect to make deep runs. That’s not a team that stinks. That’s a team that was a couple of plays away.

But that’s the double standard. Expectations drive the narrative. If you’re expected to be good, a hiccup gets an excuse. If you’re expected to be bad, a close loss becomes proof. And that’s what’s been frustrating about some of these national voices for a long time.

But this week? They surprised me. No Lamar excuse. No asterisk. Instead, they did something that every Jaguars fan has been waiting for. They started breaking down the tape.

And here’s the thing you have to understand when you watch guys like Orlovsky or Brian Baldinger break down these throws, or even Kurt Warner, who I’ve seen do it too. Trevor is releasing the ball before his receivers come out of their breaks. He’s throwing to spots, trusting the timing, trusting the play design, trusting his guys.

And I’ve got to give some credit to Nick Wright, too. Nick Wright gets on my nerves sometimes, I’ll be honest, but he’s been riding the Trevor train for a long, long time. He was on that train even during the rough patches, when maybe he should’ve pulled over and filled up the tank. But the bigger point is this. When a former player, especially someone who played the quarterback position, starts gawking over how a guy throws the football, it’s not just about the throw. It’s about the mindset. It’s about where that player is mentally. Former quarterbacks understand that in order to even make some of these plays, you have to be in a completely different headspace. The Tight End Room Is Paying Off

And while we’re talking about weapons, let’s talk about the tight end room, because this is one of my favorite things about the start of this game.

The Jaguars opened the game by going to Tanner Koziol. Now, I hadn’t seen Koziol do much all year, because he’s basically their fourth tight end. It was even mentioned on the broadcast. Their fourth tight end! And the Jaguars came out and made him the very first target of the game.

And remember all the criticism? So many people were asking, “Why are you taking so many tight ends in the draft? Why are you investing in that position?” Well, last year was a strong draft class for tight ends, and the Jaguars didn’t have a first-round pick. So they said, “You know what, we’re going to get a couple of these guys.” And that’s exactly what they did.

So they hit Koziol on the first play. And then on the very next play, they roll out and hit Nate Boerkircher, and Nate starts dragging defenders. I honestly didn’t think they were going to get him on the ground. And as I’m watching this, I’m thinking, oh yeah, this is all hands on deck. Everybody you think should be making plays is making plays. Everybody you want to make a play is making a play.

That’s the huge weapon right there. It’s not one guy. It’s depth. It’s a roster where the fourth tight end can step up on national television and make a play. It’s a roster where the coaching staff trusts everybody on the field. And when you have that kind of depth and that kind of trust, defenses can’t just key in on one guy.

And I’ll tell you what, I’ve got to wonder about those first two plays. I don’t know for sure, but I have to wonder if there was a moment during the week where the coaches said, “Hey, we’re on national TV. Let’s show everybody exactly how wrong they were about our draft board. Let’s show them what these guys can do.”

Now, I never want players or coaches going out there just to prove a point. That’s not how you win football games. But if it falls in line with the game plan, and you get the chance to do it? Do it.
