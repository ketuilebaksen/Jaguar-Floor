Great News Number One: Jessie Bates Is a Jaguar

Let’s start with the first move, the one that kicked off this whole frenzy. The Jacksonville Jaguars traded for safety Jessie Bates from the Atlanta Falcons, sending a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick to Atlanta.

Let that sink in for a second. Jessie Bates. One of the top playmaking safeties in the entire National Football League is now wearing teal and black.

This is a team that’s six and two, that just went into Baltimore and handled business 33 to 10, and that is now pushing its chips into the middle of the table for a deep postseason run. That’s exactly what this move is. It’s a statement. It’s the Jaguars saying, “We’re not just happy to be here. We’re here to win now.”

And here’s what I find really interesting. Even analysts who cover the Falcons came out and said this was a good trade for both teams. And that’s something we don’t talk about enough in this league. Both teams can win a trade.

From Atlanta’s side, the logic makes sense. The Falcons are three and six. They’re looking to replenish their draft stock for 2027. And when you look at what other teams have gotten for safeties around this age, the Falcons did well. Bates turns 30 this coming February. Over the last few years, when teams traded safeties like Minkah Fitzpatrick or Kevin Byard, the return was usually day-three picks. So for Atlanta general manager Ian Cunningham, getting a second-round pick for Bates was the kind of value he simply couldn’t pass up.

Atlanta loses a leader on their defense, a playmaker, and a huge part of the defensive turnaround they’ve had over the last few years. But they gain a draft pick that, if they hit on it, could help them build toward being the kind of team the Jaguars are right now.

And that’s the key phrase right there. The kind of team the Jaguars are right now. Because Jacksonville is the team other franchises are looking at and saying, “Maybe one day we can be in that position.”

Now, from Jacksonville’s side, this is about winning football games in January and February. Jessie Bates brings instincts, ball skills, and leadership to a secondary that needed exactly that. He’s the kind of safety who changes how opposing quarterbacks read the field. He’s the kind of player who turns a good defense into a scary defense. And for a team that’s already winning, adding a player of his caliber could be the difference in a playoff game that comes down to one big play.

And here’s a little bonus that gets me excited. Think about what could happen next season. Antonio Johnson is going to be a free agent. Jessie Bates could be re-signed. Is it possible that next year, we see Jessie Bates and Antonio Johnson lined up side by side in this secondary? Nothing’s a lock. But man, that sounds nice.

Great News Number Two: Jordan Davis Joins the Trenches

Now let’s talk about the second big move, and honestly, this is the one that gets me the most fired up. Because this is the trenches. This is where football games are won and lost. The Jaguars went out and acquired defensive tackle Jordan Davis.

Jordan Davis is 26 years old. He’s a massive, powerful presence in the middle of a defensive line. And remember, this team has been dealing with injuries up front. DaVon Hamilton has been out. Travon Walker has been dealing with a rib issue. Against Baltimore, we saw the Ravens try to take advantage of that by getting the ball out quickly and attacking the short and intermediate areas. The interior of this defensive line needed reinforcement. And the Jaguars went out and got it.

Now, I know some people look at this trade and immediately focus on the draft picks. They see first-round pick and second-round pick and they start panicking. “Why would you give up that much?” I hear you. But I want you to take a step back and look at the full picture, because once you do, you’ll see that this is an outstanding deal.

Let’s talk about the money first, because this is a big part of it. Jordan Davis has roughly 600,000 dollars left on his salary for this season. That’s it. Six hundred thousand dollars for a 26-year-old defensive tackle in his prime. And then his contract runs about 10 million the following year and 15 million after that.

For a player of his caliber at his age? That is a bargain. That is a steal. And the Jaguars got him without having to hand him any big bonus money up front.

And here’s another thing. Because Davis is only 26, he’s going to be in Jacksonville well beyond the timeline of those draft picks. By the time any player taken with those picks would be fully developed and ready to contribute at a high level, Jordan Davis would still be in his prime, anchoring this defense. The Jaguars aren’t renting a player here. They’re investing in a cornerstone.

Why Those Draft Picks Aren’t What You Think

Let’s dig a little deeper into the draft pick conversation, because this is where a lot of people get it wrong.

Folks see “first-round pick” and “second-round pick” and they treat them like they’re the same as the number five overall pick. They’re not. Not even close.

Think about where the Jaguars are likely to be picking. This team is six and two. They’re on pace to run away with the AFC South. They could very well end up as one of the top seeds in the AFC. If they win the Super Bowl, that pick is number 32. And even if they don’t, realistically, we’re talking about somewhere around 28 or 29 at worst.

So let’s be honest about what that means. When a team is picking 28th or 29th, front offices sit down with their director of college scouting and their entire scouting department and ask a simple question: “At that spot in the draft, what kind of player are we realistically looking at? Can that player help us more than Jordan Davis?”

And the answer is no. It’s just what it is. The answer is absolutely not. A late first-round pick is a lottery ticket. Jordan Davis is a proven, 26-year-old difference-maker. That’s not a hard decision.

And there’s another layer to this. The Jaguars have a strong track record of developing players they don’t take in the first round. They’ve shown they can bring in players from day two and day three and turn them into real contributors. Why? Because this organization places so much emphasis on alignment, on fit, and on culture. Other teams might just stack their board based on consensus rankings. The Jaguars do it differently.

Look at Nate Boerkircher. A lot of people laughed at that pick when it happened. Nobody’s laughing now. Because everybody has seen what he can do on the field. Remember him dragging defenders against Baltimore? That’s the kind of player this front office finds where other teams aren’t looking.

So when you trade away a late first and a second-round pick, you’re not trading away your future. You’re trading away picks in a range where this front office has already proven it can find value later in the draft anyway.

DaVon Hamilton is going to be a free agent after this season. Great News Number Three: How Gladstone Pulled It Off

And now we get to the third piece of great news. And this is the one that people really aren’t talking about enough. Because James Gladstone did something remarkable here, and most people don’t even realize it.

Picture the team you saw take apart the Ravens in Baltimore. Now imagine adding three players to that roster in the middle of the season. And here’s the incredible part: the Jaguars didn’t have to cut anybody. They didn’t have to restructure anybody’s contract. They didn’t have to move money around or create cap space through some complicated maneuver. None of that.

How is that possible? Let’s break it down.

Jordan Davis only has about 600,000 dollars left on his salary for this season. And with Jessie Bates, the Atlanta Falcons are paying 6.75 million dollars of his remaining money. Atlanta is eating that cost.

Think about what that actually means. Both of those teams had already paid these players money and given them guarantees. And now they’re eating a big chunk of it without getting the on-field return on that investment. Why? Because the Jaguars were willing to give them draft picks in return. In a sense, those teams essentially bought draft picks from the Jacksonville Jaguars, and they paid for them by covering the salary.

That is creative, smart, modern roster building. That’s a general manager who understands how to use every tool available.

And this is where I think we’re seeing a shift in how some teams operate in this league. The game has changed on the field over the years, and it’s changing off the field too. The way smart teams think about draft capital, salary, and roster construction is evolving. And James Gladstone is right at the front of that evolution.
