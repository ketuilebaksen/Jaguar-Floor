The Bombshell: Jacksonville Is Clearing Cap Space

So here's what happened. After the trade deadline had already passed, the Jaguars went to work on their salary cap. They added void years to Foye Oluokun's contract, which spreads his cap hit further down the road. And they created more room through Walker Little's contract as well.

Now, when a team clears cap space after the trade deadline, people immediately start asking questions. What's this for? Are they about to add a veteran? Are they going to scoop up one of the players getting released around the league? And there are definitely players hitting the market. The Saints just released safety Justin Reid, who used to play for the Kansas City Chiefs.

But I don't think that's what this is about.

I think the Jaguars are identifying players they know are going to be part of this football team for years. Not just this season. Not just next season. Years beyond that. And they're making room to take care of them.

Kicking the Can, the Smart Way

Now, I know what some of you are thinking. Void years? Restructures? Isn't that just kicking the can down the road?

And yes, technically, it is kicking the can down the road a little bit. But here's the big difference. It's not dangerous when the players you're doing it for are actually productive. These aren't bad contracts the Jaguars are trying to hide. These aren't guys the team is desperate to get rid of while pushing the money into the future and hoping for the best.

We've seen the bad version of that in Jacksonville before. There was a time when the Jaguars did this with contracts like Roy Robertson-Harris's, and it didn't work out the way they hoped. That's not what's happening now.

What this front office is doing is using these tools on players they know are going to be here for a long, long time. And even when you get to those void years and the money comes due, the salary cap will have risen by then. The cap goes up every single year. So money that looks big today looks a lot smaller a couple of years from now.

Jessie Bates and Jordan Davis Were Their Free Agency

And here's why. You could look at it like this: Jessie Bates was their free agency. Jordan Davis was their free agency. They just used draft capital to get them instead of cap space in March.

Now, I know some of you are going to say, "Wait a minute. How can you call it free agency when they gave up draft picks? Free agents don't cost you anything except money." Fair point. But hear me out.

First, the Jaguars got these players in a season where they have a real, legitimate shot at winning the Super Bowl. In free agency, you're signing players for next year, and you don't know what next year looks like. These guys are helping right now, during a title run.

Second, at least with Jordan Davis, the Jaguars are getting him while he's already under contract. That means there's no big signing bonus they have to pay up front. And there's no leverage on the player's side to demand more money.

Why Up-Front Money Matters So Much

And this is something more fans need to understand, because it explains so much about how NFL teams operate. It's not always about the salary. It's about the guaranteed money up front.

Look around the league. In Tampa, Vita Vea held out because of his contract situation. Dexter Lawrence pushed the Giants to trade him because they didn't want to give him all that money up front. And now the Giants are out there looking for defensive line help at the trade deadline. That tells you how much these contract mechanisms matter.

Teams basically say, "We don't mind paying you the salary. We just don't want to hand you a huge pile of guaranteed money up front." That's what it comes down to.

And that's exactly why these trades worked for Jacksonville. With Jordan Davis, the Eagles already paid him the big bonus money. The Jaguars are only paying him around 600,000 dollars for the rest of this season, and his contract next year is a bargain based on his ability. Philadelphia is the team that's out that money, because they paid it up front.

And with Jessie Bates, the Falcons agreed to pay part of his salary. That's important. I guarantee you that if Atlanta hadn't covered part of that money, the price would have looked different, because the Jaguars didn't really have enough cap room to absorb his full salary. Especially with Jordan Davis coming in at the same time.

The Bidding War With Buffalo

And here's the detail that really puts it all into perspective. It turns out that, in hindsight, the Jaguars were in a bidding war with the Buffalo Bills for both Jessie Bates and Jordan Davis.

Think about that. The Bills, one of the biggest threats in the AFC, wanted both of these players. And the Jaguars beat them to it. So now you understand why Jacksonville moved so aggressively. They weren't just adding talent. They were keeping that talent away from a direct playoff rival.

And because they did it this way, through trades instead of free agency, they're now in a position to spend on retaining their own people. And that brings us to the most important name on this list.

Why This Approach Works

Let's step back and look at the whole strategy, because it's actually brilliant when you put it together.

Instead of throwing big money at outside free agents every March, where you're competing with the entire league and often overpaying, the Jaguars are doing three things.

First, they're trading for proven players who are already under contract, where someone else has already paid the bonus money. That's how they got Jordan Davis and Jessie Bates.

Second, they're using smart cap tools like void years and restructures on productive players they know will be here long term. That's what they just did with Foye Oluokun and Walker Little.

And third, they're using the space they create to keep their own core players, like Parker Washington, Jessie Bates, Antonio Johnson, and Ventrell Miller, before those players ever reach the open market.

Add in a young core with Trevor Lawrence, Travis Hunter, Travon Walker, Brian Thomas Jr., and Anton Harrison, all in their mid-twenties, and a salary cap that keeps rising every year, and you've got a team that's built to contend for a long, long time.

This is the decision that's going to define the Jaguars' future. Not one splashy signing. A disciplined, intelligent approach to building and keeping a championship roster.
