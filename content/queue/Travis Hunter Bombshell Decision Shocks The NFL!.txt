Why Davis and Bates Are Sitting

Liam Coen dropped the news on Friday, and the reason is something most people never would have thought about. Neither Jordan Davis nor Jessie Bates has had a single bye week this season.

Here’s how that happened. When the trades went through, the Eagles and Falcons were still in the middle of their schedules. Neither had hit their bye yet. So Davis and Bates went straight from their old teams to Jacksonville without a single day off. And the Jaguars had already had their bye earlier in the season.

Now add it up, and it gets scary. If both of these guys played every game from here on out, they’d each end up playing 18 regular-season games, with the playoffs still to come. That’s a massive physical load, especially for a 340-plus-pound defensive tackle.

And on top of that, they’re trying to learn a brand-new defense in just a few days. New terminology, new teammates, new calls, new responsibilities.

So Liam Coen made the call. He wasn’t going to run them into the ground. Neither player practiced on Wednesday or Thursday. And when the team went out in full pads, Davis and Bates stretched and then walked back inside. Coen’s plan is simple: rest them, teach them, and bring them back fresh for the stretch run.

And honestly, I love this decision. It shows a coaching staff that’s thinking about January and February, not just one game in November. These players weren’t brought here to win one game against a one-win Titans team. They were brought here to win in the playoffs. And you want them at full strength when it matters most.

Not Every New Face Is Sitting

Now, not all the new additions are taking the week off. Davon Godchaux, the third defender Jacksonville added, will play against the Titans, because the Saints had already given him his bye week. So he’s fresh and ready to go.

And running back Bhayshul Tuten is set to play after missing early-week practice. That shouldn’t surprise anybody. Last week against the Ravens, on a short week, with no practice at all, he still ran 12 times for 29 yards and a touchdown. This guy shows up no matter what.

The Jaguars can afford to play it safe this week, because this team is already rolling.

This Defense Was a Nightmare Before the Trades

Here’s something that should scare every other team in the AFC. Before any of these trades, this Jaguars defense was already a nightmare to score on.

Just look at this. Only one team has scored more than 20 points against Jacksonville all season long. Just one. And four other teams couldn’t even reach double digits.

Now here’s the strange thing. If you just look at the yardage numbers, they don’t jump off the page. Jacksonville is fifth against the run, which is good, but 22nd against the pass, which is nothing to brag about.

But the game completely changes when the field gets short. Inside the 20-yard line, this is a top-ten defense. On third down, this is a top-ten defense. Offenses can march up and down the field all afternoon, and then the drive just dies right when it matters. That’s the identity of this unit. Bend, but don’t break. And when it’s time to make a stop, they make it.

The Ball Finds This Defense

And the ball just seems to find this group. Jacksonville leads the entire NFL in turnover margin at plus nine. And those plays come from everywhere.

Jourdan Lewis leads the team with three interceptions. Up front, Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen keep constant pressure on opposing quarterbacks. Behind them, the linebacker who leads the team in tackles also holds up in coverage, which almost never happens in today’s NFL.

And then there’s Ventrell Miller, who is now being mentioned alongside guys like Fred Warner when people debate the best linebacker in football. Liam Coen says Miller is playing with more patience and better vision, and he still hits hard when he gets there.

But I want to focus on one name in particular, because he’s the reason I’m so confident about where this team is headed.

Travis Hunter: The Championship X-Factor

Travis Hunter has two interceptions and six pass breakups this season. And he’s doing all of that while still playing offense.

Let that sink in for a second. In today’s NFL, where players are more specialized than ever, Travis Hunter is making plays on both sides of the football. That’s not supposed to be possible. Nobody does that. And he’s not just surviving on defense. He’s making game-changing plays. He’s breaking up passes. He’s taking the football away.

And this is why I believe Travis Hunter is going to be the player who brings a championship to Jacksonville.

Think about what wins in the playoffs. It’s the big moment. It’s the one play that flips a game. A pick in the red zone. A deep catch on third and long. A momentum-shifting turnover in the fourth quarter. And Travis Hunter is a player who can deliver those moments on either side of the ball. In a single game, he can take an interception away from you on defense and then turn around and make a huge catch on offense. How do you game plan for that?

Remember, the Jaguars moved up in the draft to get him. A lot of people questioned that trade. A lot of people said it was too much. But this is exactly why you make that move. Because players like Travis Hunter don’t come around often. Players who can change a game in every phase are the kind of players that championship teams are built around.

And here’s the part that really got my attention this week. Travis Hunter made a point about how the new defensive line helps him and the rest of the secondary. With Jordan Davis and the Duval Wall pushing the pocket, there’s going to be more pressure on opposing quarterbacks. And more pressure means more rushed throws. More bad throws. And more bad throws mean more chances for the secondary to take the ball away.

That’s Travis Hunter understanding exactly how this defense is built to win. He knows that when the front four collapses the pocket, the ball is going to come out early and off target. And when it does, he’s going to be there.

So when you put it all together, you’ve got a defense that already leads the league in turnover margin, a new front that’s going to force even more bad throws, and a two-way star in Travis Hunter who lives for those moments. That’s a recipe for a championship.

Anthony Campanile’s Old-School Blueprint

The man calling this defense is coordinator Anthony Campanile, and his plan is as old school as it gets. Stop the run first. Then, once the other team starts to panic and throw, let the defensive backs go make plays.

Just look at what happened against the Colts. A 42 to 7 win that was over before the fourth quarter even started. This group isn’t built to look pretty on a stat sheet. It’s built to squeeze you for 60 minutes until you break.

But a defense like this only works if the middle holds up. And this one just lost a key piece.

Rebuilding the Middle

Jacksonville was ranked number one against the run until DaVon Hamilton went down with a knee and quad injury. His season is over, and the unit dropped from first to fifth almost overnight. Then safety Eric Murray went down with an elbow injury, and he’s done for the year too.

Most teams would have patched the holes and hoped for the best. James Gladstone did the opposite. In one day, he landed three starters, and the league is calling the new group the Duval Wall.

Jordan Davis takes Hamilton’s spot. He came from Philadelphia for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. He stops the run, he wrecks passing plays, and he’s already won a Super Bowl, so January doesn’t scare him.

Jessie Bates takes Murray’s spot. He came from Atlanta for a 2027 second and a 2028 fifth. That’s a steep price for a safety, but he’s one of the best in the league.

And Godchaux was the bargain. The Saints got a conditional 2027 sixth-round pick, and the Jaguars got a nose tackle to fill the exact spot they lost.

Davis even talked about using pocket push to keep quarterbacks from stepping up, which should help the edge rushers finish plays. And again, that’s exactly what Travis Hunter was talking about. Push the pocket, force bad throws, take the ball away.

The timing says everything. The Jaguars hold a three-game lead in the AFC South, and they’re chasing the number one seed in the AFC. They didn’t trade for depth. They traded to win it all this year.

Liam Coen Is Making History

Still, a great defense only gets you so far. The real engine of this team sits on the other side of the ball, and it starts with a head coach who’s doing something only two other men in NFL history have done.

Liam Coen is 19 and 6 as head coach of the Jacksonville Jaguars. That includes a 13 and 4 season and a division title last year, for a franchise that was a punchline around the league for most of a decade.

And here’s the historic part. Coen’s teams scored 702 points in his first 25 games. That’s one of the fastest starts of the Super Bowl era. Only two head coaches got to 700 points faster. Mike Martz did it in 22 games with the Greatest Show on Turf. Sean McVay did it in 23. And Coen actually worked under McVay with the Rams.

His big breakthrough came in 2024 in Tampa Bay, where he took Baker Mayfield, a quarterback two teams had already moved on from, and turned him into a 41-touchdown, 4,500-yard star.

How Coen’s Offense Works

So how does this offense work? Coen mixes in a lot of tricks. Jacksonville uses a ton of motion and a lot of play action, but they don’t throw much on early downs. They’d rather run it and let their formations open up the field.

A third of their snaps include two or three tight ends, and every one of them is a real threat. And the motion does something sneaky. Before the snap, the defense has to move, and how it moves tells the quarterback what coverage is coming. The game gets easier for the quarterback before the ball is even snapped.

Coen also fixed a slow-start problem. Last year, Jacksonville scored on its opening drive just three times in 18 games. This year, they’ve already done it three times in eight games. Why? Because the coaches now give players a short list of calls to hunt for on that first drive.

And the locker room has completely bought in. Jessie Bates said that’s what stood out to him most in his first week. He pointed to the urgency in the team meetings and how clear the message is.

Coen’s staff is also back together for a second year, which he says matters more than people realize. Last year’s win in San Francisco gave this team the belief that it can beat anyone, anywhere. And Coen wants that belief to stick, even with the big new names in the building. His message to the team was blunt: nobody gets to sit back and wait for the new guys to make the plays.

Trevor Lawrence: From Doubted to MVP Talk

That attitude has helped one player in particular, a quarterback who used to hear nothing but doubt. Two years ago, people wanted Trevor Lawrence gone. Now he’s firmly in the MVP conversation.

Trevor has 18 touchdown passes and only four interceptions, with a 111.7 passer rating. He leads the league in yards per attempt, adjusted yards per attempt, and yards per completion. His touchdown rate is 8 percent, which works out to a touchdown on about one of every 12 or 13 throws.

Last season was already strong, with 29 touchdowns and 12 interceptions, and he closed it out by throwing 12 touchdowns without a single interception. But this year is a completely different level.

What makes his season so unusual is the combination. He’s completing more than 65 percent of his passes, and he’s also top ten in how far downfield he throws. Most quarterbacks have to choose between being accurate and being aggressive. Trevor is doing both.

And his rough years were never all his fault. He came out as the most hyped quarterback prospect in a decade, then landed with Urban Meyer, and after Meyer was fired, with Doug Pederson. Neither coach found the answer. The talent was always there. It just needed the right environment to shine.

An Offense Built to Cash In

The offense around Trevor is built to cash in. Jacksonville is fourth in the league in points but only 13th in yards. That tells you they don’t waste trips near the end zone.

And Trevor has plenty of help. Parker Washington leads the team with 493 receiving yards and five touchdowns. Tuten has 521 rushing yards. Jakobi Meyers is in the mix. And of course, Travis Hunter is a weapon on this side of the ball too. When drives stall, kicker Cam Little steps in and bails them out.

Last week, the whole machine rolled over Baltimore 33 to 10. But there’s a catch worth noting. The Ravens played without Lamar Jackson and still ran for 162 yards. That’s something this defense has to clean up, and it’s another reason getting Jordan Davis fully integrated is so important.

Jordan Davis Feels Right at Home

Now let’s get to some of the best stories from the week, because the new guys have been great.

Jordan Davis walked into the Jaguars’ building just a few weeks after playing against them, and he found an old friend waiting for him. Travon Walker is his old buddy from Georgia, and Davis said being around him feels like school again, except the classes are meetings now. His locker sits right between Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen, and the three of them already have a handshake going.

Davis also joked about the field goals Cam Little kicked against his Eagles. He said it stung back then, but it’s funny how the league works.

On the playbook, he said it’s the same defensive tree he knew in Philadelphia, just with new words and a lot more fronts.

As for leaving Philly, Davis said he had an emotional moment on the way to the airport and played Frank Sinatra’s “My Way.” He said he holds no bad blood. He grew up in Charlotte and wants to give back, because he remembers how he felt as a kid when Cam Newton showed up at his school.

Davon Godchaux: Adapt or Die

Davon Godchaux had a very different Monday. He was at home in New Orleans when Saints head coach Kellen Moore called to tell him he’d been traded to Jacksonville. Godchaux asked if he could come the next day, and he was told no. The team needed him right away.

So he flew from New Orleans to Houston, and then to Jacksonville, landing just before Jordan Davis did. He leaned on a saying from his old coach Brian Flores: adapt or die. And when he finally saw Davis in person, he laughed and said the guy made him feel small. When a 330-pound man says you make him feel small, you know you’re a big human being.

Jessie Bates and the Viral Moment

Jessie Bates had it even rougher. He landed from Spain and didn’t sleep at all. He told reporters he’s been sleeping about five hours a night since then, and he even stayed at the building late into the night learning the defense.

He also got emotional about leaving Atlanta. He talked about teary eyes from coaches, trainers, and equipment staff, and he specifically mentioned the cafeteria workers and janitors as people he’ll miss. That tells you a lot about the kind of person Jessie Bates is.

And then there’s the story that went viral. Bates told his young nephews about his new team, and the very first thing he mentioned was that they’d get to see him play with Travis Hunter. The video blew up online, and Travis Hunter promised to send the young boy a jersey.

Think about that. Even NFL veterans, players who’ve been in the league since 2018 and have more interceptions than anyone over that stretch, are excited to play alongside Travis Hunter. That’s the kind of star power and respect Hunter already commands. And that’s the kind of player who elevates everyone around him in a championship run.

What It All Says About This Locker Room

Another Jaguars player, not one of the new guys, said he had no contact with anybody about the deadline except his agent. And a teammate believes the best is still ahead for this offense.

Jordan Davis by the Numbers

And when you look at Jordan Davis’s career and contract, it’s even more eye-opening. He was a star long before he got to Jacksonville. He’s 26 years old. At Georgia, he was an AP All-American who won both the Outland Trophy and the Bednarik Award. Those honors go to the best interior lineman and the best defensive player in college football.

The Eagles loved him so much that they traded up to take him with the 13th pick in 2022. His first deal was a four-year contract worth about 17 million dollars. Philadelphia then picked up his fifth-year option, which pays about 12.9 million dollars, fully guaranteed, for this season. And in March, the Eagles gave him a three-year, 78-million-dollar extension that kicks in next year. Now Jacksonville is the team that gets to enjoy that investment.
