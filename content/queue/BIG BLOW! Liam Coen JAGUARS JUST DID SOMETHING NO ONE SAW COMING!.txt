The Decision No One Saw Coming

Let’s start with the news itself. Liam Coen announced that Jordan Davis and Jessie Bates III are both out for Sunday’s game against the Titans. And the reason is something most people never would have thought about.

Neither player has had a single bye week this season.

When the trades went down, the Eagles and Falcons were still grinding through their schedules. Neither team had reached its bye. So Davis and Bates went straight from their old teams to Jacksonville without one day off in between. And the Jaguars had already taken their bye earlier in the season, coming back from London.

Do the math, and it gets concerning. If both players suited up for every game the rest of the way, they’d each end up playing 18 regular-season games. And that’s before the playoffs even begin. For a defensive tackle who weighs over 340 pounds, and a safety who flies around the field on every snap, that’s a heavy workload.

And on top of the physical toll, they’re trying to learn a brand-new defense in a matter of days. New terminology. New teammates. New calls. New communication on every single snap.

So Coen made a decision. Neither player practiced on Wednesday or Thursday. When the team went out in full pads, they stretched and walked back inside. The plan is simple: rest them, teach them, and bring them back fresh.

Now, I understand the frustration. You make a blockbuster trade, you want to see your new guys on the field right away. That’s human nature. But think about why these players are here. They weren’t brought to Jacksonville to beat a struggling Titans team in November. They were brought here to win games in January and February. And the best way to make sure they’re at full strength for those games is to give them this week.

That’s a coach thinking three moves ahead. That’s a coach who understands the long game. And honestly, it tells you just how confident this staff is in the rest of the roster.

Who Is Playing

Now, not every new face is sitting out. Davon Godchaux, the third defender the Jaguars acquired, is expected to play, because the Saints had already given him his bye week. So he’s fresh and ready to anchor the middle.

And when Davis is ready to go, the updated depth chart tells you a lot about how this defense is going to look. A lot of people were wondering whether Arik Armstead would lose his starting job. The answer is no. Arik Armstead is still going to start, right next to Jordan Davis, with Davon Godchaux backing them up. That’s a rotation that’s going to keep everybody fresh and keep opposing offensive lines exhausted.

And here’s more good news on the back end. Antonio Johnson is back. He returned last week after missing one game, and once Jessie Bates is cleared to go, you’re going to see the two of them back there together. That’s this secondary at full strength.

And think about how different that is from the end of last season, when the secondary was beaten up and thin. Right now, you’re looking at the Jaguars at their absolute best. And once the new pieces are fully up to speed, that’s when you want the communication on the back end to be flawless.

The Mission: Play to the Standard

Now let’s talk about this game against the Titans, because the mission here is bigger than just the final score.

Nobody expects the Jacksonville Jaguars to go into Nashville and lose to the Tennessee Titans. Let’s be honest about where Tennessee is right now. They’re banged up. They’re depleted. They weren’t a good team even when they had everybody healthy. They’ve traded away a couple of their best defenders. And some of their other top players are going to be out with injuries.

But the Jaguars still have a mission. And the mission isn’t just to win. The mission is to play a certain way.

Look, I know the coach-speak. “We don’t apologize for wins. As long as we get on the plane and come home with a W, we’re happy.” And that’s true. A win is a win. But the bottom line is, you want to see this team finally string together a run of games where everything is clean. Where the execution is sharp from start to finish. Where there are no sloppy stretches, no lapses in focus, no letting the opponent hang around longer than they should.

Think about the path this team has taken. They came back from London. They came back off the bye. And they put together a very dominant performance against the Colts, a 42 to 7 win that was over before the fourth quarter. Then they went into Baltimore on a short week and won 33 to 10.

Now it’s time for the next step. Because that’s what great teams do. Great teams play a certain way every single week, no matter who’s on the other side.

It’s almost like a computer program. Once you reach a certain level, there’s a way you go about your business. You can’t just turn it on for the big games and coast through the rest. If you’re going to be a championship team, if you’re going to be the kind of team people are now saying the Jaguars are, there’s a standard of play that comes with that territory. And that standard has to show up every week.

First, Brian Thomas Jr. The Jaguars held on to him at the trade deadline, at least for the rest of this season, and they’ve talked about him in glowing terms. The coaching staff has said he’s going to be important down the stretch. Well, now is a perfect time for him to get on a nice little run.

And remember, there are only 17 games in the regular season. Every one of them matters. Even when a game looks like a foregone conclusion, you don’t want to treat it like one. Third, the pass rush. Everybody keeps saying this team doesn’t have a pass rush. The Jaguars only have 16 sacks on the season, and that’s the one area where they’ve been below where they want to be. It’s Travis Hunter Time

And now let’s talk about the player I’m most excited to watch on Sunday. Because I’m telling you right now, it’s Travis Hunter time.

Travis Hunter has already had a game this season where he picked off a pass and scored a touchdown in the same afternoon. Think about how rare that is in the modern NFL. One player, making a game-changing play on defense and then finding the end zone on offense. That’s not supposed to happen. But that’s Travis Hunter.

And this season, he’s got two interceptions and six pass breakups, and he’s doing it while still playing on offense. Two-way football at the highest level of the sport.

But here’s the thing. I want to see even more. I want to see Travis Hunter have a big, explosive game. A breakout game. The kind of game that makes the whole country stop and say, “Okay, now I get it. Now I understand why the Jaguars traded up to get him.”

Because a lot of people questioned that trade. A lot of people said the Jaguars gave up too much to move up in the draft. But this is exactly the reason they did it. Players like Travis Hunter don’t come around very often. And when they do, you go get them.

And I truly believe that if Hunter keeps building on what he’s done so far, he has a real chance to put himself in the conversation for first-team All-Pro. That’s how special he is. And a game against a depleted Titans team, with a quarterback who’s going to be under pressure all day, is a perfect stage for Travis Hunter to make that statement.

Think about how this defense is going to create chances for him. When the pass rush collapses the pocket, the ball comes out early. It comes out off target. And when that happens, Travis Hunter is going to be waiting. That’s exactly the point he made himself this week: more pressure on the quarterback means more bad throws, and more bad throws mean more chances for the secondary to take the ball away.

And when Jessie Bates is back on the field next to him, it’s only going to get better. Bates has 28 career interceptions, tied for the most in the league since he entered in 2018. You put Bates, Antonio Johnson, Jourdan Lewis, and Travis Hunter all in the same secondary, and opposing quarterbacks are going to have nightmares.

Remember the story from this week? Jessie Bates told his young nephews about his new team, and the very first thing he mentioned was that they’d get to see him play with Travis Hunter. Even a veteran like Bates is excited to line up next to him. That tells you everything about the respect Hunter has already earned around the league.

So on Sunday, keep your eyes on number 12... well, keep your eyes on Travis Hunter wherever he lines up. Offense, defense, it doesn’t matter. Because when the moment comes, he’s going to be right in the middle of it.

Why Coen’s Decision Makes This Team Stronger

Let’s circle back to the big decision one more time, because I want to make sure everyone understands why this is a good thing.

When Liam Coen sits two healthy starters he just traded big draft capital for, he’s sending a message to the entire locker room. He’s saying, “We trust the guys who got us here.” He’s saying, “We’re not panicking over one game.” And he’s saying, “We’re building for the biggest games of the season.”

That’s the same mindset that has made this team so successful. Liam Coen is 19 and 6 as head coach in Jacksonville. He led this team to 13 and 4 and a division title last year. His teams have scored 702 points in his first 25 games, one of the fastest starts in Super Bowl-era history. Only Mike Martz and Sean McVay got there faster.

This is a coach who knows what he’s doing. And when he makes a decision that surprises people, history tells us there’s a good reason behind it.

And the defense he’s protecting is already one of the best in football. Only one team has scored more than 20 points against Jacksonville all season. Four teams couldn’t even reach double digits. This unit is top ten in the red zone and top ten on third down. It leads the NFL in turnover margin. Ventrell Miller is being mentioned alongside Fred Warner as one of the best linebackers in the league.

So even without Davis and Bates for one week, this defense is more than capable of handling the Titans. And when those two do return, rested, refreshed, and fully up to speed on the playbook, this defense is going to be even more dangerous heading into December.

Looking Ahead

And don’t forget, the Jaguars play the Titans again in just a couple of weeks. So this game is also a chance to set the tone for the rematch. Beat them convincingly now, and you plant that seed in their heads before you see them again.

The schedule down the stretch is very manageable, according to the people who break down strength of schedule for a living. The Jaguars’ remaining opponents have a combined winning percentage of just .418. That’s one of the easiest remaining slates among AFC contenders.

But here’s the thing. An easy schedule only matters if you take care of business. And taking care of business starts on Sunday in Nashville.

The Jaguars hold a three-game lead in the AFC South. They’re chasing the number one seed in the AFC. Home-field advantage throughout the playoffs is right there for the taking. And every game, including this one, is a step toward that goal.
