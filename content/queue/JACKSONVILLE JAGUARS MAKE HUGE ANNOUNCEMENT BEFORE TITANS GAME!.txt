Why the Jaguars Went Shopping

To understand these trades, you have to understand what happened to this defense. Two key players went down, and both are done for the season. Defensive tackle DaVon Hamilton, one of the best run stoppers in football, is out for the year. And safety Eric Murray is out for the year too.

And you could see the impact almost immediately. The Jaguars had the best run defense in the entire NFL. In just one week, they dropped all the way to fifth. That’s how important those two players were.

So the front office had a decision to make. Sit tight and hope the depth holds up through the rest of the season and into the playoffs? Or go out and get reinforcements? And this front office doesn’t sit tight. That’s just not who they are.

Trade Number One: Jessie Bates III

The first deal brought in safety Jessie Bates III from the Atlanta Falcons. The price was a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick.

Jessie Bates is a ball hawk. Throughout his career, he has 28 interceptions and 14 forced fumbles. In fact, since he entered the league back in 2018, he’s tied for the most interceptions of any player in the NFL. Let that sink in. Over that stretch, nobody has more picks than Jessie Bates.

And here’s why that’s so exciting for this specific defense. The Jaguars are already fourth in the NFL in takeaways. They’re first in the entire league in turnover differential at plus nine. They’ve already intercepted 11 passes this season. So you take a defense that already loves taking the football away, and you add one of the best ball hawks of the last decade? That’s frightening for opposing quarterbacks.

Trade Number Two: Davon Godchaux

The second deal was quieter, but don’t overlook it. The Jaguars brought in defensive tackle Davon Godchaux from the New Orleans Saints, and it only cost them a sixth-round pick.

Godchaux is six-foot-three and 330 pounds. He’s a ten-year veteran and a pure run stopper. He’s not going to be on the highlight reels every week, but he’s the kind of player who makes life miserable for opposing running backs. He plugs holes. He eats blocks. He holds the point of attack. And for the price of a sixth-round pick, that’s outstanding value for a team trying to rebuild its run defense on the fly.

Trade Number Three: The Shocker, Jordan Davis

And then came the shocker. The deal that had the entire league buzzing. The Jaguars traded for defensive tackle Jordan Davis from the Philadelphia Eagles. And this one came with a big price tag: a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick.

Jordan Davis is six-foot-six and over 340 pounds. The Eagles took him 13th overall back in the 2022 draft. And last season, he posted career highs with 72 tackles and four and a half sacks. He’s a strong run stopper, and according to Pro Football Focus, he also generated 28 quarterback pressures last year. So he’s not just a space-eater. He can get after the quarterback too.

And here’s a big reason why the price was so high. Jordan Davis is locked up through 2029 on a 78-million-dollar deal. The Jaguars aren’t renting a player for a few months. They’re getting a long-term piece in the middle of their defense.

Now, let’s be clear about where this leaves the Jaguars’ draft situation. Jacksonville now has no first-round pick and no second-round pick in 2027. But their 2028 and 2029 first-round picks are still theirs. So this isn’t a team that’s completely emptied the cupboard. But it is a team sending a very clear message.

The time is now.

This is the kind of risk that either turns a team into a champion or sets it back for years. There’s no in-between with a move like this. And I love that this front office had the courage to make it.

Not Everyone Loved It

Of course, not everybody was a fan of the Jordan Davis deal. ESPN gave the Jaguars a very bad grade for the trade. And plenty of people around the league questioned whether giving up a first-round pick for a defensive tackle was worth it.

But James Gladstone fired back. He said the Jaguars got a premier player with a well-rounded game. And he has the evidence on his side. A run stopper who also generated 28 quarterback pressures last season? A 26-year-old under contract through 2029? That’s not a reckless move. That’s a calculated one.

Now, the honest question is whether this deal ends up being a steal or a mistake. Only time will answer that. But I’ll tell you this: I’d much rather have a front office that swings big while the window is open than one that sits on its hands and wastes a season like this.

The Timing Could Not Be Better

And here’s why the timing of these moves makes so much sense. The AFC doesn’t have a clear king right now.

Think about it. For years, there was always one dominant team in the conference that everybody had to go through. Right now? Nobody has separated. Six AFC teams already have at least six wins. That’s a crowded race at the top. And in a race that tight, every single game matters.

Right now, Jacksonville sits as the third seed in the conference. And this team is pushing hard to take the lead before somebody else grabs it.

And the oddsmakers have taken notice. When the season started, the Jaguars were plus 1,200 to win the AFC. That’s long-shot territory. Now? They’re as low as plus 285. That puts them ahead of the Buffalo Bills, who sit at plus 310. That is an enormous jump in just nine weeks. Las Vegas doesn’t move numbers like that unless they genuinely believe a team is for real.

The Prize: Home-Field Advantage

So what’s the prize the Jaguars want most? Home-field advantage throughout the playoffs.

And here’s a stat that should get every Jaguars fan fired up. Jacksonville hasn’t had home-field advantage since 1999. That’s how long it’s been.

And this would be a total game changer, because the road through the AFC playoffs is brutal. Kansas City, Denver, Pittsburgh, Buffalo. These are some of the toughest places to play in the entire league in January. Freezing temperatures. Snow. Wind. Loud, hostile crowds.

And here’s the thing: the Jaguars are the only warm-weather team in the mix. Nobody wants to spend the playoffs traveling to frozen stadiums. But if Jacksonville can grab that top seed, they might never have to leave home until the Super Bowl. Every opponent would have to come to Duval. And I like the sound of that a lot.

The Schedule Gives Them a Real Shot

And the schedule gives the Jaguars a legitimate chance to make it happen. Their remaining opponents have a combined winning percentage of just .418. That’s the second-easiest remaining schedule among projected AFC playoff teams, and the 30th easiest in the entire league.

The Jaguars play the Titans twice. They also face the Giants, the Cowboys, and the Commanders. Now, there are some tough games mixed in. A road trip to Chicago to face the Bears. A road game against the Giants. And a home game against the Pittsburgh Steelers. And December can always get tricky. But overall, this is a schedule a team like the Jaguars should be able to take advantage of.

Meanwhile, in Philadelphia

Now, while Jacksonville is celebrating, the team on the other side of the Jordan Davis trade is going through the exact opposite.

The Philadelphia Eagles are a mess right now. They’re two and seven. They’re in last place in the NFC East. They’ve lost seven straight games, with the latest coming Sunday in a 19 to 11 loss to the Giants. And things got even worse, because quarterback Jalen Hurts is out with blood clots.

So for Philadelphia, the season is basically over. Trading away a starting defensive lineman was an easy decision. They’re already looking ahead to the future, and this deal loaded them up for it. The Eagles got an extra first-round pick next April and an extra second-rounder in 2029. They now hold ten picks in the first two rounds of the next three drafts, including two first-round picks in both 2027 and 2028.

For a team that just fell apart, that’s a big pile of options. Eagles fans may hate the losing, but their front office just bought itself a lot of room to rebuild.

And that’s why this trade makes sense for both sides. One team is rebuilding. One team is chasing a championship. Each got exactly what it needed.

The Offense Made This Gamble Possible

Now, here’s something really important. The biggest reason the Jaguars felt comfortable making this gamble is the offense. This offense is giving the defense all the room in the world to work with.

The Jaguars have scored 30 or more points five times this season, and they’re four and one in those games. The only loss came against Houston, by just three points. They’ve also won by 20 or more points four times, including their last two games. First they beat Indianapolis, and then they crushed Baltimore 33 to 10.

Their point differential is plus 108. That’s the best in the entire NFL. When a team is scoring like that, it can afford to take a few big swings on the other side of the ball.

Trevor Lawrence: MVP Level

And the man behind it all is Trevor Lawrence. Since Liam Coen took over as head coach, he has turned Trevor into an MVP-level quarterback.

Look at these numbers. Over his last 25 regular-season games, Trevor Lawrence has thrown for nearly 6,000 yards, with 47 touchdowns and 16 interceptions. That’s elite production.

And this season? He’s thrown for 1,932 yards, averaging 8.6 yards per attempt. His passer rating sits at 111.7, which is one of the best in the entire league. He’s accurate, he’s aggressive, and he’s making throws that have former quarterbacks around the country breaking down his film in awe.

The Run Game Is Doing Its Part Too

And the run game is pulling its weight as well. The Jaguars are in the top half of the NFL, averaging 117.1 rushing yards per game. And they’re doing it without a true superstar running back.

Bhayshul Tuten and Chris Rodriguez Jr. are both averaging more than four yards per carry. And Tuten is a real threat as a pass catcher too, giving Trevor another reliable option out of the backfield. That balance makes this offense so hard to defend. You can’t just sit back and take away the pass, because they’ll run it right at you. And you can’t stack the box, because Trevor will carve you up through the air.

The Coaches Are Getting Noticed

All of this success is putting the spotlight on the coaching staff. Offensive coordinator Grant Udinski and defensive coordinator Anthony Campanile are both expected to get head coaching interviews this coming cycle. Some people even believe Minnesota’s offense has struggled since Udinski left to come to Jacksonville.

That’s what happens when you win. Other teams come calling for your coaches. And while it might mean the Jaguars face some staff changes down the road, it’s also a sign of just how well this team is being coached right now.

The Duval Wall

Now let’s talk about the part of this team that just got a massive upgrade. The defense. And it’s got a new nickname to match. They’re calling it the Duval Wall. And this isn’t just a slogan.

Remember, even before these trades, the Jaguars’ defense was giving up just 15.1 points per game. That’s second in the entire league, behind only Minnesota. And now they’ve reinforced it.

Look at this front. Jordan Davis and Davon Godchaux in the middle. Arik Armstead rotating in to keep everybody fresh. And Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen rushing off the edges. That’s a big, physical, powerful defensive line.

With Godchaux being a pure run stopper and Davis being a massive presence in the middle, opposing offenses aren’t going to find much room on the ground. And that matters a lot in the AFC, because Buffalo, Pittsburgh, and Kansas City all lean on their run games. If you want to beat those teams in January, you have to stop the run. And now the Jaguars are built to do exactly that.

The Wall Should Help the Pass Rush

And here’s where it gets even more interesting. The Duval Wall should also help the pass rush, which has been this team’s one real weak spot. The Jaguars have only 16 sacks this season. That’s simply not enough for a team with championship aspirations.

But think about what happens now. With two huge defensive tackles in the middle, opposing offensive lines have to commit extra blockers inside. And that means Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen should get a lot more one-on-one opportunities on the edges. The Jaguars might not even need to blitz much, because four rushers could be enough to get home.

And the linebackers are going to love it. Ventrell Miller and Branson Combs are going to benefit, because the defensive linemen will keep blockers off of them, letting them flow freely to the football. And Anthony Campanile now has a ton of different ways to call a game.

The Back End

Then there’s the back end. The secondary now has Jessie Bates, Jourdan Lewis, and Travis Hunter. That’s a group with veteran savvy, ball skills, and explosive athleticism. And again, combine that with a defense that’s already fourth in takeaways and first in turnover differential, and you’ve got a unit that can flip a game with one play.

The One Risk

Now, there is one risk here, and I want to be honest about it. Adding three new starters at once is a lot to absorb in the middle of a season. These guys have to learn the playbook, learn the terminology, and build chemistry with their new teammates.

But the good news is the timing works out. The Titans twice and the Giants are coming up in the next three games. That gives these new players time to settle in before December, when the schedule gets tougher and every game carries playoff weight.

And there’s a question that might never get a clear answer. Would the Jaguars have made the Jordan Davis deal if DaVon Hamilton were healthy? We may never know. But the Jessie Bates trade was happening either way. Bates was an upgrade regardless of injuries.

The Front Office Philosophy

What is crystal clear is that Jacksonville’s front office has a very specific way of building this team.

James Gladstone says this was not an all-in move. He sees it as a chance to get better, and he’s not treating draft picks like they’re sacred. That’s a philosophy he learned from his former boss, Les Snead of the Los Angeles Rams, who has shown over the years that you can win at the highest level without hoarding high picks.

And Gladstone’s view is simple. A first-round pick from a playoff team is not the same as a first-round pick from a terrible team. Bad teams pick in the top ten. Winning teams pick near the end of the round. So Jacksonville isn’t giving up nearly as much as it looks like on paper.

There’s a pattern to this plan. The Jaguars want players who are already proven, so they don’t have to wait years for them to develop. And many of these players have already been paid their big signing bonuses by someone else.

That’s also why Jacksonville was so quiet in free agency. Part of it was old dead money on the books from the previous regime. But the rest of it was a different way of thinking. They’d rather trade for a player during the season and sign him later than fight every other team for him in March. They did exactly that with Jakobi Meyers, who was extended before he ever reached free agency. And Jessie Bates is expected to follow the same path. Plus, the salary cap goes up every single year, so there will be room.

And the core of this team is young. Trevor Lawrence, Travon Walker, Brian Thomas Jr., Anton Harrison, and Travis Hunter are all in their mid-twenties. Remember, Jacksonville moved up in the draft to get Hunter. That’s why this window could stay open for five to seven years, not just this season.

The front office also knows what it is and what it isn’t. When a move doesn’t work, like last year’s deadline deal, they cut it loose and move on. And they didn’t chase a big name like Bobby Brown just to make noise. Every move had a purpose.

And head coach Liam Coen sets the tone with a simple “let’s go” mindset. He even has a story about Jordan Davis. Back when Coen was at Kentucky, he watched Davis walk the field before a game and thought his offense wasn’t going to gain a single yard against him. Now that same Jordan Davis is on his team.

The Huge Announcements: BJ Green and Brian Thomas Jr.

Now let’s get to the announcements every Jaguars fan has been waiting for. Even with all this buying, there were two more pieces this team needed. One who’s coming back, and one who almost got traded away.

The first piece is BJ Green. Liam Coen announced that his return window has officially opened, so he could be back on the field soon. And that matters a lot, because the Jaguars did not trade for an edge rusher at the deadline. They called the Jets about Will McDonald, but New York wouldn’t sell. So BJ Green is the pass rush help, especially on third down. He isn’t the long, twitchy type of rusher. He’s a guy who simply finds a way to get to the quarterback. And with only 16 sacks so far, this team needs exactly that.

The second piece is Brian Thomas Jr. He stayed in Jacksonville, and the team says he’s going to be a big part of the second half of the season. Coen has been straight with everybody about that, and it would have taken a huge offer to pry him loose. So the Jaguars kept every one of their offensive weapons while still adding three defensive starters. That’s a win-win.

The Weak Spots

To be fair, this team does have a few weak spots. Kick returns are the biggest issue. Young guard Emmanuel Pregnon has given up some pressure, and the pass blocking can get shaky against good pass rushers. That could matter in the playoffs, when every opponent is strong.

But honestly, there’s not much else to pick at. The coaches seem to know exactly what this team can and can’t do. This team went 13 and 4 last year, and a lot of people expected a drop-off. It didn’t happen. At six and two, they might lose no more than four games all season. Some voices around town have even predicted 15 and 2. And the only thing that could really slow them down is injuries.

How Far This Franchise Has Come

Take a moment and think about how far this franchise has come. Just 18 months ago, owner Shad Khan fired head coach Doug Pederson, and plenty of fans questioned the direction of the team. Now? Jacksonville is one of the best teams in the National Football League.

Some fans still can’t quite believe it, and that’s understandable after so many years of losing. But my advice is simple. Enjoy it. Don’t rush the ending. Soak in every single win.

Next Up: The Titans

And the next test is a familiar one, with some old pain attached. On Sunday, the six and two Jaguars head to Nissan Stadium to face the one and seven Tennessee Titans. It’s Jacksonville’s first divisional road game of the season.

On paper, it looks like a gift. Tennessee has just one win, and the Jaguars have beaten them four straight times. Jacksonville has won seven of the last ten meetings. And the Jaguars’ 28 wins over the Titans are the most they have against any team in the NFL.

Under Liam Coen, Jacksonville is 15 and 4 against AFC opponents and 6 and 2 against the AFC South. This is a team that knows how to win inside its conference.

But Jaguars fans know better than to relax. Back in 1999, Jacksonville was one of the best teams in football, and every single one of its losses that year came against the Titans. That’s the kind of pain that sticks around.

And Tennessee is a team with nothing to lose. A bad team can be dangerous when it has nothing left to play for. There’s also talk that the Titans could be sellers, so they may be building more for next year than this one. Still, a loss to a one and seven team would sting, and it would put a real dent in the plan for the top seed. This is a must-win.
